\begin{thesisabstract}{english}

Multi-step LLM workflows pay for every token they read, and context
compression is the obvious lever. The single-agent
prompt-compression literature is mature, but it measures
single-fragment question-answering, while production workloads
combine information across multiple compressed fragments. Whether
single-agent QA accuracy predicts that harder property has not
previously been tested.

It does not. On a new $150$-instance multi-fragment benchmark
spanning cross-document fact aggregation, constraint-satisfaction
planning, and multi-step retrieval, four training-free compressors
at ten compression ratios yield workload-level Spearman correlations
between QA-F1 and coordination success in $[-0.82, +0.32]$, with all
$95\%$ bootstrap CIs excluding $0.6$. The single-agent benchmarks
that dominate the compression literature therefore systematically
mis-rank compressors for multi-fragment deployment.

The mechanism is a sharp \emph{coordination cliff} $\tau^{*}$,
present on $11$ of $12$ (compressor, family) cells. A
compounding-error model derives the threshold structure from a
per-family recall constraint on critical tokens and recovers the
empirical position to first order (median $\approx 35\%$ relative
error). Within a calibrated regime the cliff shows no detected
shift between a local Llama-3.1-8B planner and a frontier
Qwen-2.5-72B planner across a $9\times$ scale-up and a change of
architecture family; extended-reasoning planners sit outside this
regime. Compression also reduces inference disclosure of protected
facts, but in proportion to how aggressively each compressor
destroys source tokens rather than through privacy-specific
filtering.

Three pre-registered hypotheses (H1, H2, H4) are supported as
stated. Two (H5, H6) sharpened during analysis into
\emph{Corollary~1 (Ceiling--Cliff Separation)} and
\emph{Corollary~2 (Information-Density Scaling)}, both supported on
their refined criteria. H3 narrowed from a regime sign-flip
prediction to a single-stack compress-first ordering. The
deliverable is the manuscript and an open-source reference
implementation of a policy-enforcing memory bus, reproducible from a
single command given a precomputed compression cache.

\end{thesisabstract}
