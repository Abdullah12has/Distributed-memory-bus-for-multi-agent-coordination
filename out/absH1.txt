Six hypotheses are tested across tens of thousands of evaluation cells (approximately $52\,000$ — see Chapter 4 for the per-experiment counts). **H1** (single-agent question-answering accuracy is not a transferable predictor of coordination success under compression) is supported: at the workload level (one measurement per workload, $n=150$; the earlier pooled $n=1350$ over-counted the nine ratios per workload as independent) Spearman $\rho \in [-0.82, +0.32]$ across compressors, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals excluding 0.6 for all four. The striking finding is that the pooled correlation is largely an artifact: LLMLingua-2 and truncation, which look moderately positive when pooled ($\rho\approx+0.38$), collapse to near-zero at the workload level ($\rho=+0.03$ and $+0.05$, CIs spanning zero), while the instruction-aware filter is strongly negative ($\rho=-0.82$) and Phi-3-Mini extractive moderately positive ($\rho=+0.32$). The filter's overall negative value is moreover a *between-family* effect: within each family it is weak or undefined (family-a $\rho=-0.13$, n.s.; families b and c degenerate because coordination collapses uniformly, leaving no rank variance). Across the twelve (compressor, family) cells the only statistically significant within-family workload-level correlation is *negative* (LLMLingua-2 on family-c, $\rho=-0.46$, $p=7\times10^{-4}$). The honest H1 conclusion is therefore not "compressors disagree on a consistent sign" but the stronger methodological point that QA-F1 change does not positively predict coordination change for any compressor at the workload level, and that the pooled single-number correlations are inflated/distorted by pseudo-replication and between-family pooling. **H2** (a sharp cliff exists) is supported on eleven of twelve (compressor, family) cells with paired Wilcoxon $p < 0.0001$. The original **H5** is reframed as **Corollary 1 (ceiling-cliff separation)**, stated as *no cliff-position shift was detected across the heterogeneous planners tested, on the cells where the cliff is resolvable* rather than as proven invariance. Model capacity sets the success ceiling $p_0$; the cliff position $\tau^{*}$ is set by the compressor and task threshold $\theta_q$. The local cross-architecture sweep at \{Qwen-2.5-1.5B, Phi-3-Mini-3.8B, Llama-3.1-8B\} shows a $24\%$ tau spread on family-c — within the $50\%$ tolerance the validator originally shipped with but *failing* the strict $20\%$ tolerance that matches the H2 spec. The strongest single test is therefore one frontier cell: Qwen-2.5-72B cliffs $7.3\%$ from the canonical synthetic reference $\tau^{*}=2.5$ (inside its bootstrap CI). DeepSeek V4 Pro's point estimate is $\tau^{*}=2.15$ ($13.8\%$ below the reference), but its bootstrap CI is $[1.76,7.14]$ with a bootstrap *median* of $5.7$ — far from the $2.15$ point estimate — so the cliff position is only weakly identified and CI-containment of the reference is weak evidence (it is not an equivalence test, and the CI would contain almost any cliff position in the swept range). The corollary should be read as supported on one reasonably-identified cell (Qwen-72B), consistent on the local arm, and not yet established at the strict tolerance. The original **H6** is reframed and supported as **Corollary 2 (information-density scaling)**: the *shape* of the cliff (sharpness and pre-cliff plateau) varies with task information density, with $\theta_\text{info}=0.97$ on a dense numeric-aggregation family and $0.37$–$0.48$ on distributed multi-document benchmarks (HotpotQA and MultiHopRAG). **H4** (compression reduces inference disclosure) is supported for three of four compressors with two documented caveats: (i) the Llama-3.1-8B reader is YES-biased so the metric tests asymmetric leakage; (ii) the disclosure-reduction ranking is monotonic in *how aggressively each compressor destroys source tokens* — truncation (most destructive) leads at $24.6$ pp, the token-level filter and LLMLingua-2 follow at $20.4$ and $18.9$ pp, and Phi-3-Mini extractive (verbatim-span preserving) shows no significant reduction ($0.4$ pp, $p = 0.91$). H4 thus measures information destruction rather than privacy-specific filtering; the metric remains useful as a deployment-time disclosure-budget mechanism but the privacy interpretation is calibrated against the destruction baseline. The thesis also contributes **CAAC** — a cliff-aware adaptive compression wrapper that operationalises the compounding-error bound as a runtime back-off, populating a complementary operating point on the (coordination, ratio) frontier.
