16:This thesis studies that question on *multi-fragment LLM workflows*. The empirical evaluation uses either a deterministic regex parser or a single LLM call with all (compressed) fragments visible as the planner; multi-round agent simulation is outside the empirical scope. Four contributions are delivered: **(C1)** a reproducible coordination benchmark of 150 instances across three workload families — cross-document fact aggregation, constraint-satisfaction planning, and multi-step retrieval; **(C2)** an empirical characterisation of the *coordination cliff* $\tau^{*}$ across four training-free compressors (LLMLingua-2, Phi-3-Mini extractive, an instruction-aware filter, and a truncation baseline), with a compounding-error model that *explains the existence and sharpness* of the cliff and gives a *first-order* estimate of its position from per-round critical-token recall (median relative error $\approx 35\%$; the model's bootstrap band quantifies sampling rather than specification error and does not contain the empirical position — §4.4.4, §5.3), together with a cross-architecture consistency check on Qwen-2.5-72B (cliff position $7.3\%$ from the synthetic reference, inside its bootstrap CI) and DeepSeek V4 Pro (point estimate $78\%$ from the reference but with a CI wide enough to contain it — corroborating but underpowered) inside a formally-defined calibrated regime; **(C3)** a catalogue of three RAG pipelines showing that, *on the single retriever/embedder stack evaluated here* (`bge-large-en-v1.5` + FAISS-CPU), compress-first is preferred over retrieve-first in both storage-bounded and accuracy-bounded regimes — a result that does not reproduce the post-retrieval-compression preference of the LongLLMLingua line of work on this stack (we do not claim to falsify it in general). A joint relevance-conditional pipeline scores higher F1 still, but the EUR cost model in its current form emits *identical* per-pipeline cost breakdowns (§4.7), so no cost-parity claim is made for that comparison; and **(C4)** a memory-bus reference implementation built around a tamper-evident audit log and a policy-aware compressor abstraction, together with a summary-level *inference-disclosure* metric that quantifies how compression reduces the recoverability of protected facts to a downstream reader.
18:Six hypotheses are tested across tens of thousands of evaluation cells (approximately $52\,000$ — see Chapter 4 for the per-experiment counts). **H1** (single-agent question-answering accuracy is not a transferable predictor of coordination success under compression) is supported: at the workload level (one measurement per workload, $n=150$; the earlier pooled $n=1350$ over-counted the nine ratios per workload as independent) Spearman $\rho \in [-0.68, +0.47]$ across compressors, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals excluding 0.6. The H1 finding is that the *sign and magnitude* of the QA–coordination relationship are compressor-dependent (filter $\rho=-0.68$ — strong anti-correlation; LLMLingua-2 and truncation $\rho\approx+0.47$ — moderate positive), so no single F1-based selection rule transfers across compressor families. A per-family decomposition (§4.2) localises the sign disagreement to the constraint-satisfaction family-b (filter $-0.74$ vs the others $+0.33$ to $+0.45$); family-a is degenerate for every compressor (coordination collapses uniformly, leaving no rank variance) and on family-c only the filter retains coordination variance (correlating $-0.60$) — so the pooled correlation must not be read as a uniform across-task mechanism. **H2** (a sharp cliff exists) is supported on eleven of twelve (compressor, family) cells with paired Wilcoxon $p < 0.0001$. The original **H5** is reframed as **Corollary 1 (ceiling-cliff separation)**, stated as *no cliff-position shift was detected across the heterogeneous planners tested, on the cells where the cliff is resolvable* rather than as proven invariance. Model capacity sets the success ceiling $p_0$; the cliff position $\tau^{*}$ is set by the compressor and task threshold $\theta_q$. The local cross-architecture sweep at \{Qwen-2.5-1.5B, Phi-3-Mini-3.8B, Llama-3.1-8B\} shows a $24\%$ tau spread on family-c — within the $50\%$ tolerance the validator originally shipped with but *failing* the strict $20\%$ tolerance that matches the H2 spec. The strongest single test is therefore one frontier cell: Qwen-2.5-72B cliffs $7.3\%$ from the canonical synthetic reference $\tau^{*}=2.5$ (inside its bootstrap CI). DeepSeek V4 Pro's point estimate is $\tau^{*}=4.45$, a $78\%$ gap from the reference; its CI contains the reference only because the CI is very wide ($[1.76,7.14]$), so it is corroborating-but-underpowered evidence (CI-containment is not an equivalence test), not independent confirmation. The corollary should be read as supported on one strong cell, consistent on the local arm, and not yet established at the strict tolerance. The original **H6** is reframed and supported as **Corollary 2 (information-density scaling)**: the *shape* of the cliff (sharpness and pre-cliff plateau) varies with task information density, with $\theta_\text{info}=0.97$ on a dense numeric-aggregation family and $0.37$–$0.48$ on distributed multi-document benchmarks (HotpotQA and MultiHopRAG). **H4** (compression reduces inference disclosure) is supported for three of four compressors with two documented caveats: (i) the Llama-3.1-8B reader is YES-biased so the metric tests asymmetric leakage; (ii) the disclosure-reduction ranking is monotonic in *how aggressively each compressor destroys source tokens* — truncation (most destructive) leads at $24.6$ pp, the token-level filter and LLMLingua-2 follow at $20.4$ and $18.9$ pp, and Phi-3-Mini extractive (verbatim-span preserving) shows no significant reduction ($0.4$ pp, $p = 0.91$). H4 thus measures information destruction rather than privacy-specific filtering; the metric remains useful as a deployment-time disclosure-budget mechanism but the privacy interpretation is calibrated against the destruction baseline. The thesis also contributes **CAAC** — a cliff-aware adaptive compression wrapper that operationalises the compounding-error bound as a runtime back-off, populating a complementary operating point on the (coordination, ratio) frontier.
137:**C2 — Empirical characterisation of the coordination cliff and a compounding-error model that explains it.** *The cliff exists and is sharp; its position shows no detectable shift across the heterogeneous planners we could test, on the cells where it is resolvable, within a defined calibrated regime.* The empirical work covers four training-free compressors — LLMLingua-2 [Pan 2024], the Phi-3-Mini extractive span selector, an instruction-aware filter, and a truncation baseline — at ten compression ratios across three families and across three local planners drawn from **three different architecture families** (Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Phi-3-Mini-3.8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; the original "Qwen-1.5B/3.8B/8B" notation conflated three architectures and is corrected here), with a frontier consistency arm on Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct and DeepSeek V4 Pro: Qwen-2.5-72B cliffs within $\sim 7\%$ of the canonical synthetic reference $\tau^{*}=2.5$ (and inside the bootstrap CI on $\tau^{*}$) — the strongest single test — while DeepSeek V4 Pro's point estimate is $78\%$ from the reference, its CI containing the reference only by virtue of being very wide, so it corroborates weakly rather than confirming. (The local three-architecture sweep on family-c shows a $24\%$ tau spread, below the $50\%$ tolerance the validator originally shipped with but above the strict $20\%$ tolerance — necessary but not sufficient; the load-bearing evidence is the cross-architecture frontier arm. The local arm is itself a cross-architecture invariance result across Qwen, Phi-3, and Llama, not a within-family parameter-count sweep.) The compounding-error model derives the *threshold structure* of the cliff — why a sharp transition exists at all — from per-round **critical-token-recall** and a per-family recall threshold $\theta_q$, and recovers the empirical position only to first order (33% of cells within $\pm 25\%$, median relative error 35%; its bootstrap band does not contain the empirical $\tau^{*}$ on the testable cells — §4.4.4, §5.3). The model's contribution is the derivation of the threshold mechanism, not a tight position predictor. The same machinery supports **Corollary 2 (information-density scaling)**: the cliff *shape* varies with a task's information density $\theta_\text{info}$ (an AUC-based per-task quantity, distinct from $\theta_q$), validated on HotpotQA and MultiHopRAG against the C1 family-a synthetic reference. An out-of-regime diagnostic on GPT-oss 120B identifies extended-reasoning planners as a structurally distinct case that the model's threshold-success assumption does not cover — which is itself a contribution, since prior compression work treats reasoning regime implicitly.
818:The cliff position shows **no detected shift** across the three local architecture families tested (Qwen-2.5-1.5B, Phi-3-Mini-3.8B, Llama-3.1-8B) within a precisely-defined calibrated regime (§4.5) — though the local arm's 24% spread fails the strict $\pm 20\%$ tolerance, so this arm is consistent-with but not sufficient-for the claim. The load-bearing evidence is a single frontier cell: on family-a × LLMLingua-2, Qwen-2.5-72B cliffs at $\tau^{*}=2.68$, $7.3\%$ from the canonical synthetic reference $\tau^{*}=2.5$ and inside its bootstrap CI — a 48× scale-up from the 1.5B planner with no detected shift. (Two honest qualifications: the local resolvable cells are family-c while this frontier cell is family-a, so the "48× scaling" spans different task families; and DeepSeek V4 Pro's point estimate is $\tau^{*}=4.45$, $78\%$ from the reference, its CI containing 2.5 only because the CI is very wide — corroborating weakly, not confirming, §4.6.) The defensible claim is therefore "no shift detected on the resolvable cells, strongest on one frontier cell," not "invariant." The cliff is *not* invariant to reasoning regime: extended-reasoning planners such as GPT-oss 120B cliff at substantially higher ratios because they can recover from sub-threshold information by chain-of-thought, violating the threshold-success assumption A3. This is the empirical evidence that the cliff is a property of the (compressor, task) pair within a defined regime — the structural finding that the thesis contributes to the compression literature.
